The Three-Body Program · Delivery Lab

Your developers write code faster. Can the rest of your delivery system keep up?

The Three-Body Delivery Lab brings the people who plan, build, review, govern and release software together around one real change. In two or three focused days, they do the work, expose the new constraints and leave with a workflow they can repeat.

The trigger

The queue simply moves somewhere else.

Agentic coding can compress implementation from a sprint to a few days. Planning, review, testing, governance and deployment rarely move at the same speed, and the AI policy rarely reaches the pipeline at all. The result is not automatically faster delivery. The queue simply moves somewhere else.

This is not another prompt workshop. We find the new constraint by running real work through the whole system.

How it works

One real change, the whole delivery system, two or three days.

01

Prepare one real outcome

We select a bounded work item, bring in only the people needed to complete it and make access and decision paths ready.

02

Execute together

Over two or three focused days, the team takes the work from intent through implementation, review and governance to an agreed definition of done. Along the way, we decide who owns which rule.

03

Make the path repeatable

We leave the team with the working result, the evidence of how it was governed, the blockers and decisions made visible, and a 30-day experiment for its normal delivery flow.

What you leave with

Completed work, visible constraints, a workflow you can run again.

  • One real change completed or made production-ready.
  • The new delivery bottleneck identified with evidence.
  • Clear boundaries for AI assistance and human judgment.
  • An explicit workflow the team can run again.
  • A 30-day experiment with owners and measures.

Fit

Who this is for, and who it is not.

Good fit

Teams already using coding assistants or agents, with one real use case and leaders willing to make the relevant participants available. Regulated or complex organizations where review, governance and cross-team dependencies cannot simply be removed, and have to keep working as the code speeds up.

Not a fit

Introductory AI training, a generic hackathon, a tool demonstration, or an enterprise rollout without a real first use case.

Questions

The ones that come up first.

Is this training?

Participants learn while completing real work, but the outcome is not course completion. It is a working result and a delivery pattern they can repeat.

Do we need to replace Scrum with Kanban?

No. The Lab does not prescribe a framework. It uses evidence from the real workflow to determine which policies and flow practices should change.

Does the whole organization need to participate?

No. Start with the smallest set of people who can take the selected work to done. Expansion is a later decision.

Can this work in a regulated environment?

Yes, when security, risk and compliance are designed into the definition of done and the relevant decision-makers participate. The Lab does not bypass controls to create a faster demo.

What happens before the live days?

Focused preparation selects and slices the use case, establishes the baseline, confirms participants and prepares access, environments and decision paths.
